EXPERIMENTAL SKILLS 15.1

Does caffeine increase heart rate? Investigating with Daphnia

Daphnia (water flea) is transparent — you can watch its tiny heart beating under the microscope. Choose a caffeine solution, watch the heart (the pulsing red dot), and click the counter every time you see a beat for 15 seconds. Repeat for each concentration.

Real-lab care: Daphnia are living animals — return them to the pond water afterwards, and keep concentrations low. Ethics apply to invertebrates too.

1. Describe the effect of caffeine on heart rate. What type of drug is caffeine?

Heart rate increases with caffeine concentration. Caffeine is a stimulant — it speeds up the transmission of signals in the nervous system.

2. Why use 0% caffeine as one of the treatments?

It is the control — it shows the natural resting heart rate, so any increase can be attributed to the caffeine and not to handling or the microscope light warming the slide.

3. Suggest one limitation of counting heart beats by eye.

The heart beats fast, so miscounting is easy (human error). Better: video the Daphnia and count in slow motion, or repeat and average.

Alcohol and reaction time

1. What is the effect of alcohol on reaction time — and why is it dangerous for drivers?

Reaction time gets longer (slower). A driver with slowed reactions brakes later — at 70 km/h, an extra 200 ms means the car travels about 4 more metres before braking even starts.

2. Alcohol is called a depressant. Does that mean it makes people sad?

No — “depressant” means it slows nerve signal transmission in the brain and spinal cord, slowing reactions and reducing coordination and judgement.

3. Which organ breaks down alcohol, and what can long-term heavy drinking cause?

The liver. Long-term heavy drinking can cause cirrhosis (scarring of the liver). Alcohol is also addictive and damages judgement and coordination.
