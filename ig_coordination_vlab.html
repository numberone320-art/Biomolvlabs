A typical human reaction time to a visual signal is about 200–250 ms. F1 drivers manage ≈ 180 ms!

1. Trace the path of the impulse in this test: eye → …? → finger muscles.

Receptor cells in the eye → sensory neurone → CNS (brain) → motor neurone → effector (muscles in the hand) → response (fingers click). Unlike a simple reflex, the brain is involved because you consciously decide to click.

2. What happened to your reaction time with distraction? Explain.

It got slower (longer) — the brain must share attention between two tasks, so the signal takes longer to process. This is why using a phone while driving is so dangerous.

3. Why do we do several trials and average them?

Single measurements vary randomly (blinks, anticipation). Repeats and a mean make the result more reliable and let us spot anomalous trials.

Put the pupil reflex arc in order — click the steps in sequence:

Your sequence:

stimulus (bright light) → receptor (retina) → sensory neurone → CNS → motor neurone → effector (circular muscles of the iris) → response (pupil constricts). This protects the retina from damage by bright light.

2. Which iris muscles contract in bright light — and what do antagonistic muscles do in dim light?

In bright light the circular muscles contract (radial muscles relax) → pupil gets smaller. In dim light the opposite: radial muscles contract, circular relax → pupil dilates to let in more light. Muscle pairs that oppose each other are called antagonistic.

3. Reflex vs conscious action: give one feature that makes the pupil reflex a reflex.

It is automatic and involuntary — you cannot stop your pupil constricting by willpower, and it does not involve the conscious parts of the brain. This makes it fast and protective.
