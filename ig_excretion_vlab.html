Diagnosing patients with urine test strips

Your kidneys filter the blood and remove urea, excess water and salts as urine. Doctors can spot disease from what appears in urine. Dip a test strip into each patient’s sample, read the two pads against the colour chart, and diagnose each patient.

Analysis questions

1. Glucose should never appear in urine — it is small enough to be filtered, but what normally happens to it in the kidney tubules?

All glucose is normally reabsorbed back into the blood. Glucose in urine means the blood glucose was so high the tubules could not reabsorb it all — a sign of diabetes (too little insulin).

2. Why does protein in urine suggest kidney damage?

Protein molecules are too large to pass through a healthy filter (the glomerulus). If protein appears in urine, the filter is leaking — the kidney is damaged.

3. Where is urea made, and from what?

In the liver, from excess amino acids (deamination). The amino group, which contains nitrogen, becomes urea; the rest of the molecule is respired or stored.
EXPERIMENTAL SKILLS 13.2

Dialysis tubing: a model of the kidney’s partially permeable membrane

Dialysis (Visking) tubing has tiny pores: small molecules pass through, large ones cannot — just like the kidney filter. Fill the tubing with a mixture of starch (large) and glucose (small), seal it, and place it in pure water. After 20 minutes, test the water around it.

1. Which substance passed through the tubing, and which stayed inside? Explain using molecule size.

Glucose passed out (small molecule — fits through the pores); starch stayed in (large molecule — too big for the pores). The tubing is partially permeable.

2. How is this similar to what the kidney does — and how is it different?

Similar: the kidney filters small molecules (urea, glucose, salts, water) out of the blood while keeping large ones (proteins) and blood cells in. Different: the living kidney also reabsorbs useful substances (all glucose, some water and salts) — tubing cannot do that.

3. In kidney dialysis for patients, what must be true about the dialysis fluid?

It must contain the correct levels of glucose and salts (so these are not lost from the blood) but no urea — so urea diffuses out of the blood down its concentration gradient.
