Simulated data The times come from a model with random variation, so they are realistic but not real measurements. Use them to practise the method, tables and graphs.

Aim

Investigate how pH affects the time for a catalase-soaked filter-paper disc to rise in hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂ → water + oxygen).

Variables

Independent: pH of buffer · Dependent: time to rise (s) · Controlled: 5 cm³ H₂O₂, 10 cm³ buffer, 5 × 5 mm paper, same catalase, temperature.

Set up

0.0 s
(sped up 3×)

Results table: time / s

Each run adds a try for that pH. Mean is calculated automatically. Clear anomalies by reading the table first.

Graph: mean time against pH

Questions (coursebook Experimental skills 5.1)

1 Write a word equation for the reaction.
hydrogen peroxide → water + oxygen (catalase)
2 Why does the paper rise?
catalase on the paper makes oxygen; the bubbles stick to the paper and lift it.
3 Describe the relationship between pH and time taken.
Time falls as pH rises to about 7, then rises again above 7. Quote figures with units from your table.
4 Are there any anomalous results?
An anomalous result does not fit the pattern (for example, one try far from the other two). Leave it out of the mean.
5 Estimate the optimum pH.
The pH with the shortest time (about pH 7).
6 How could you find a more precise optimum?
Test more pH values close to the estimate (for example 6.5, 7, 7.5).
7 Sources of error and improvements?
Judging when the paper reaches the top by eye (use a video or a second observer); paper squares of different size (cut accurately); different amount of catalase on the paper (soak for the same time).

Aim

Investigate how temperature affects the time for lipase to digest the fat in milk. Fatty acids lower the pH, so thymolphthalein changes from blue to colourless.

Variables

Independent: temperature of water bath · Dependent: time for the blue to disappear (s) · Controlled: 5 cm³ milk, 7 cm³ sodium carbonate, 5 drops indicator, 1 cm³ lipase, same tube.

Set up

0.0 s
(sped up 3×; stops at 300 s if there is no change)

Results table: time / s

“No change” means no colour change in 300 s.

Graph: mean time against temperature

Questions (Experimental skills 5.2)

1 Why does the blue colour disappear?
Lipase breaks fat into fatty acids, which lower the pH; the indicator is colourless in lower pH.
2 Which temperature is closest to the optimum?
The temperature with the shortest time (about 40 °C).
3 Explain the result at 60–70 °C.
Too much kinetic energy; the lipase loses its shape and the active site is no longer complementary to the fat: enzyme denatured.
4 Explain the result at 10 °C.
Low kinetic energy, so few effective collisions between enzyme and substrate (the enzyme is not denatured).
